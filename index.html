Behavioral Coherence: A Method for Sensitive-Domain LLM Evaluation

Anika Sharma, Malavika Mampally, Chidaksh Ravuru, Kandyce Brennan, Neil Gaikwad

Can a model give a reasonable answer while repeatedly making the same assumptions about people’s lives? I used a validated abortion-stigma scale to compare responses from five LLMs across 627 personas, then worked with reproductive-health experts to interpret the patterns.

↳Models often assumed people would face judgment and keep their experiences secret, even when their circumstances differed.

Size Sound Symbolism Survives Genealogy Across 27 Languages

Anika Sharma, Emma Wrenn, Tianyi Niu, Shashank Srivastava

Do word sounds carry the same meaning across unrelated languages, or does shared ancestry explain the pattern? I tested size sound symbolism across 27 languages using cross-language transfer and adversarial representation learning adapted from domain adaptation.

↳A small, mostly vowel-driven size association persisted even when models were discouraged from relying on language identity.

SOCIAL NLP · GENDER BIASFirst author · Under review at ARR

Language Models Show a ‘Women-Are-Wonderful’ Effect in Blame Attribution That is Not Just Leniency

Gender bias doesn’t always look like hostility. We asked whether LLMs judge the same interpersonal conflict differently depending on who is involved. I created matched gender variants of 11,270 Reddit conflicts to separate the gender of the person being judged from the person affected.

↳Models were somewhat more forgiving when judging women, but much harsher when the same behavior affected a woman.

HCI · REPRODUCTIVE EQUITYCHI 2026 Extended Abstract + Preprint

CARE: A Capability Framework for Reproductive Equity in Human-AI Interaction

A reproductive-health chatbot might give accurate advice, but can someone check it, understand it, or use it? We drew on the capability approach to evaluate these tools with health literacy, stigma, and barriers to care in mind.

↳Missing sources, medical jargon, and overly long or rigid answers can make even accurate information hard to question or use.

INTERACTIVE SYSTEMS · AI EDUCATIONIn development

EDNA: Interactive AI Fairness Education

Students can use AI to build an ML model without necessarily understanding why it made certain choices. I’m building EDNA to turn AI-generated code into visual blocks and use Socratic questions to help students examine modeling decisions and fairness tradeoffs.

↳Testing whether having students explain their choices to someone else improves how they reason about fairness.

HCI · AI POLICYSubmitted · FAccT

AI Voyager: Exploring AI Legislation Through Dialogue

AI bills are long, technical, and often hard to relate to everyday decisions. We built AI Voyager so people can explore proposed laws through document-grounded conversations between AI agents taking different stakeholder perspectives, and step in to question or redirect the discussion.

↳In our study, participants asked how the bills would work in practice: who they cover, what their rules mean, and how they would be enforced.

Backstory time: whales

My interest in research started with whales. During my gap year after high school, I used machine learning to classify sperm whale codas and distinguish different whale dialects. I like to summarize this work as a modest speculation about how to say hello in whale: 1 + 1 + 3.

I later worked on converting whale codas and dolphin whistles into short musical compositions. I wanted to use sound to draw attention to the issues life under the sea faces, particularly grindadráp, the pilot whale and dolphin hunts in the Faroe Islands. In May 2026 alone, 825 whales and dolphins were killed, and the Faroese parliament voted to exempt these hunts from animal welfare legislation. I thought combining art and technology might get more people interested in efforts to end the practice.

I also had a little stint trying to mint the compositions as NFTs alongside artwork of whale eyes (that sounds creepy, I swear it was artistic) to raise funds for an NGO I partnered with. That was a flop. But that’s what got me into CS!

I’m still interested in turning animal sounds into music, and one thing on my dream bucket list is to create an interactive exhibition exploring how we relate to other animals and the natural world. If you’re interested in music production, sound art, or building something like this together, feel free to email me!
